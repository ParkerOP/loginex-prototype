Phase 1 focus: intra‑city + SMEs
Target use cases: same‑city movements like retail distribution, warehouse–store shuttles, office/home shifting, and local e‑commerce deliveries using mini‑trucks and LCVs.

Value props for SMEs: standardized transparent pricing, ability to compare multiple drivers, tracking, and digital proof of delivery, which are big pain points in traditional intra‑city transport.

Driver side: prioritize owner‑operators of mini‑trucks (Tata Ace, 14‑ft, etc.) that mostly work intra‑city and need higher utilization and reduced empty runs.

Starter pricing model (intra‑city, SME)
Design pricing to be low‑commitment and transaction‑driven at first.

Shippers (SMEs)

Free tier: limited number of bookings/month, standard support, app/web only.

SME Plan (monthly): unlimited bookings, priority support, saved routes and addresses, GST invoicing, and basic analytics.

Carriers/Drivers

Free: accept jobs offered to you, basic profile and ratings.

Pro: search and bid on loads, preferred shippers, simple dispatch tools, and early payout options.

Most revenue initially comes from a small per‑trip fee or percentage of the trip value (e.g., only on completed jobs), which SMEs are used to as part of per‑km or per‑trip transport costs.

P2P, B2P, B2B in this phased approach
P2P (individual to driver)

Intra‑city: house shifting, small ad‑hoc loads; very app‑driven and purely pay‑per‑trip with upfront pricing.

B2P (SME to independent drivers)

Your core early segment: shops, warehouses, and D2C brands booking local moves with verified drivers; you monetize via per‑trip fees plus optional SME/driver plans.

B2B (business to fleets/3PLs)

Comes later: once you handle higher volumes and inter‑city lanes, add support for fleets and 3PLs, contract rates, and integrations to ERPs/TMS

PROBLEMS

Driver and UX Constraints
Feature,Reality/Constraint
Hardware,Drivers often use low-end Android devices with limited RAM/storage; heavy apps crash frequently.
Environment,"Sunlight glare, poor network connectivity, and battery drainage make real-time app usage unreliable."
Authentication,Shared phones and frequent SIM churn lead to poor account hygiene and authentication confusion.
Literacy,"Elderly or low-literacy drivers struggle with dense interfaces, necessitating multi-language and icon-based UX."

Technical and Data Challenges
Infrastructure:  GPS inaccuracies and network "black spots" in congested urban areas lead to incorrect ETAs and user frustration.
Data Hygiene:  Incomplete addresses and colloquial location names undermine the accuracy of routing and analytics.
Fraud:  The system is vulnerable to GPS spoofing, fake Proof of Delivery (POD) uploads, and collusion between drivers and shippers to manipulate mileage.
